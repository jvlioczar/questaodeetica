Some Biases That Can Influence the Choice of Strategies and How the Public Receives Them

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. Biases that affect activists themselves
  2. How the public takes in the message
  3. Individual cases or numbers?
  4. Positive and negative messages
  5. In short

Biases that affect activists themselves

People who plan actions to help animals are also subject to biases. Some examples:

  • G. I. Joe fallacy: thinking that just knowing a bias exists is enough to avoid it.
  • Optimism and pessimism bias: expecting better or worse results than the real ones.
  • Zero-sum bias: thinking that if someone gains, someone else must lose.
  • False consensus effect: thinking other people think like us.
  • Illusory correlation: seeing a link between things that aren't linked.

New word

False consensus: believing most people agree with us when they don't.

How the public takes in the message

There are also effects that show how people learn and remember:

  • Picture superiority: we remember what we see in pictures better.
  • Spacing effect: we learn better when content is repeated with breaks in between.
  • Testing effect: we remember more when we rewrite or explain something, instead of just rereading it.
  • Humor effect: messages with humor tend to be remembered more.
  • Google effect: we forget things we know we can easily find online.

Individual cases or numbers?

Since we feel more empathy for one identifiable victim, one strategy is to tell the stories of specific animals. But there's a risk: base rate neglect, which makes us give more weight to specific cases than to overall numbers. So it's good to combine the two.

Positive and negative messages

We remember negative things more, and negative things affect us more. That can help grab attention, but it can also push people away if it's overdone.

Remember

Knowing about biases helps us plan better how to talk about animals and avoid fooling ourselves about what works.

In short

  • Activists have biases too, like optimism and false consensus.
  • Knowing how people remember things helps us communicate better.
  • Individual stories and overall numbers work best together.
