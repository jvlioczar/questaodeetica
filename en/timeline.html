Timeline of ideas

129 thinkers and institutions cited in the collection, in chronological order. It shows how the debate about animals has changed.

How to read it: when we know the year of birth, it is shown; when we don't, we use the year of the person's earliest work cited in the collection. Notice how recent most of the debate is: almost all of it after 1970.

Up to the 18th century

  1. 428 BCE
    Platobirth

    In the dialogue Euthyphro he formulated the dilemma: is something good because the gods approve of it, or do the gods approve of it because it is good?

  2. 384 BCE
    Aristotlebirth

    Founder of virtue ethics; he saw animals as existing for human use, within a natural hierarchy.

  3. 341 BCE
    Epicurusbirth

    Held that death is not an evil: "where death is, we are not." The collection discusses and rejects this view.

  4. 1st c. BCE
    Lucretiusera

    Formulated the symmetry argument: we do not regret the time before we were born, so we should not regret the time after death.

  5. 1588
    Thomas Hobbesbirth

    A contractualist: morality arises from an agreement among self-interested people to avoid the war of all against all.

  6. 1632
    John Lockebirth

    Defined a person by a consciousness that extends over time, an idea used in the debate on identity and the harm of death.

  7. 1724
    Immanuel Kantbirth

    Held that only rational beings are ends in themselves; our duties regarding animals would be indirect (cruelty to them would make us cruel to humans). Contemporary Kantians such as Korsgaard argue that the theory itself implies considering animals directly.

  8. 1748
    Jeremy Benthambirth

    Wrote that the question is not whether animals can reason or talk, but whether they can suffer.

  9. 1783
    Lewis Gompertzbirth

    A pioneer: in Moral Inquiries (1824) he advocated not using animal products and was already discussing helping animals against natural harms such as predation.

19th century

  1. 1806
    John Stuart Millbirth

    In the essay "Nature" he shows that "natural" does not mean "good": nature routinely does things we would condemn in humans.

  2. 1875
    Albert Schweitzerbirth

    Proposed "reverence for life": every living being deserves respect, an idea that inspired biocentrism.

  3. 1877
    W. D. Rossbirth

    Proposed prima facie duties (fidelity, non-maleficence, beneficence, etc.); he denied that animals have rights, although he accepted duties toward them.

  4. 1887
    Aldo Leopoldbirth

    In A Sand County Almanac (1949) he formulated the land ethic: a thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability and beauty of the biotic community. The value lies in the whole, not in individual animals.

1900 to 1959

  1. 1912
    Arne Næssbirth

    Attributes intrinsic value to nature and to the diversity of life as a whole, not only to sentient beings.

  2. 1917
    William Patonbirth

    In Man and Mouse (1984) he defends animal experimentation for its medical benefits to humans.

  3. 1919
    Mary Midgleybirth

    In Animals and Why They Matter (1983) she criticizes the treatment of animals but argues that preferring one's own species is a natural bond, and to some extent a legitimate one.

  4. 1919
    R. M. Harebirth

    Proposed two-level utilitarianism: intuitive rules for everyday life and critical calculation for hard cases.

  5. 1921
    John Rawlsbirth

    In A Theory of Justice (1971) he proposed the veil of ignorance; he himself excluded animals from justice, but the impartiality test he proposed is used to include them.

  6. 1923
    Paul W. Taylorbirth

    In Respect for Nature (1986) he defends biocentrism: every living being has a "good of its own" and inherent value, whether sentient animals, plants or microorganisms.

  7. 1925
    Julian Franklinbirth

    In Animal Rights and Moral Philosophy (2005) he argues that Kant's categorical imperative, properly understood, requires treating all sentient beings as ends in themselves.

  8. 1926
    Joel Feinbergbirth

    In The Rights of Animals and Unborn Generations (1974) he argues that only those who have interests can have rights, and that animals have interests but plants do not.

  9. 1929
    Nel Noddingsbirth

    One of the founders of the ethics of care, centered on concrete relationships.

  10. 1932
    Pentti Linkolabirth

    Placed the preservation of nature above the good of humans and other animals, going so far as to advocate a drastic, authoritarian reduction of the human population. He is cited as an extreme example of holistic environmentalism.

  11. 1934
    Daniel Kahnemanbirth

    In Thinking, Fast and Slow (2011) he describes the biases and heuristics that distort our judgments, the basis of Volume X.

  12. 1936
    Michael Soulébirth

    Argued that biological diversity has value in itself and that conservation should protect species and ecosystems.

  13. 1938
    Tom Reganbirth

    In The Case for Animal Rights (1983) he argues that every "subject-of-a-life" has inherent value and rights, which rules out using them as mere resources.

  14. 1938
    Robert Nozickbirth

    In Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974) he described the position "Kantianism for people, utilitarianism for animals," which the collection criticizes.

  15. 1939
    Tibor Machanbirth

    In Putting Humans First (2004) he argues that humans have moral priority because they are moral agents, and that animals have no rights.

  16. 1941
    R. G. Freybirth

    In Interests and Rights (1980) he denied that animals have interests in the moral sense because they lack language; he defended animal experimentation.

  17. 1941
    James Rachelsbirth

    In Created from Animals (1990) he defends "moral individualism": how we treat someone should depend on the characteristics of each individual, not on species.

  18. 1941
    Jesús Mosterínbirth

    In ¡Vivan los animales! (1998) he criticizes cruelty to animals but allows hierarchies of consideration.

  19. 1942
    Derek Parfitbirth

    In Reasons and Persons (1984) he analyzed personal identity, psychological connectedness and population ethics, topics used in the debate on the harm of death and on future beings.

  20. 1943
    Bernard Rollinbirth

    Argued that animals have interests and a "telos" (a nature of their own) that must be respected, and discussed animal experimentation.

  21. 1944
    Roger Scrutonbirth

    In Animal Rights and Wrongs (1996) he claims that rights belong only to members of the moral community, who are capable of duties; to animals we owe only a certain kindness.

  22. 1946
    Mary Anne Warrenbirth

    Proposed a multi-criteria view of moral status that gives animals a lower status than persons.

  23. 1948
    Frans de Waalbirth

    Showed empathy and prosocial behavior in other primates, discussing the origins of morality.

  24. 1955
    John Harsanyiearliest work cited in the collection

    Grounded utilitarianism in impartial choice under uncertainty: what we would choose without knowing who we would be.

The 1960s and 1970s

  1. 1975
    Peter Singerearliest work cited in the collection

    In Animal Liberation (1975) he popularized the term "speciesism" and the principle of equal consideration of interests: suffering of equal intensity counts equally, no matter whose it is.

  2. 1977
    Jan Narvesonearliest work cited in the collection

    Argues that morality is an agreement among those who can cooperate and retaliate; since animals cannot, they would have no rights (Animal Rights, 1977).

  3. 1977
    Stephen R. L. Clarkearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Moral Status of Animals (1977) and in his article on "the problem of predation" he discusses whether we would have to intervene in nature.

  4. 1978
    John Bensonearliest work cited in the collection

    In Duty and the Beast (1978) he accepts duties toward animals, but of lesser weight than duties toward humans.

  5. 1978
    Philip Devineearliest work cited in the collection

    Discussed the moral basis of vegetarianism and argued that it is reasonable to give more weight to humans.

  6. 1978
    Kenneth Goodpasterearliest work cited in the collection

    In On Being Morally Considerable (1978) he argues that being alive, not being sentient, is the criterion for moral consideration.

  7. 1979
    H. J. McCloskeyearliest work cited in the collection

    Argued that only those who can claim something for themselves can have rights, which would exclude animals.

  8. 1979
    Donald VanDeVeerearliest work cited in the collection

    Argued that Rawls's original position, applied consistently, should include animals (Of Beasts, Persons and the Original Position, 1979).

The 1980s and 1990s

  1. 1980
    J. Baird Callicottearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends a holistic ethics: the good of the biotic community (ecosystems and species) is the ultimate criterion of value. In Animal Liberation: A Triangular Affair (1980) he went as far as to claim that this good can outweigh that of individuals, humans included; he later softened his position.

  2. 1980
    Thomas Nagelearliest work cited in the collection

    In Death (1970) he argues that death is an evil of deprivation; in What Is It Like to Be a Bat? (1974) he discusses the subjectivity of experience.

  3. 1981
    Ruth Cigmanearliest work cited in the collection

    In Death, Misfortune and Species Inequality (1981) she argues that death is not a misfortune for animals because they supposedly lack categorical desires about their own future.

  4. 1982
    Robert Elliotearliest work cited in the collection

    In Faking Nature (1982) he argues that original nature has a value that restored nature does not recover.

  5. 1983
    Lawrence Beckerearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Priority of Human Interests (1983) he defends the priority of human interests on the basis of social ties and closeness.

  6. 1983
    Dale Jamiesonearliest work cited in the collection

    Writes on animal and environmental ethics, including zoos and climate change.

  7. 1984
    Steve F. Sapontzisearliest work cited in the collection

    In Morals, Reason and Animals (1987) he defends animal liberation and argues that, in principle, we would have reasons to prevent predation when possible.

  8. 1985
    Daniel Dombrowskiearliest work cited in the collection

    In Babies and Beasts (1997) he analyzes the history of the argument from species overlap.

  9. 1988
    Roger Crispearliest work cited in the collection

    Works on theories of well-being and defends a form of sufficientarianism based on compassion.

  10. 1991
    Michael Leahyearliest work cited in the collection

    In Against Liberation (1991), drawing on Wittgenstein, he denies that animals have a mental life rich enough to carry the same moral weight as humans.

  11. 1991
    Cora Diamondearliest work cited in the collection

    In Eating Meat and Eating People (1978) she argues that our morality is shaped by relationships and by the idea of "fellow human beings," not by capacities; she accepts compassion for animals, but not equality.

  12. 1991
    Gary Varnerearliest work cited in the collection

    In In Nature's Interests? (1998) he argues that all living beings have "biological interests," although he gives priority to the interests of conscious beings.

  13. 1991
    Fred Feldmanearliest work cited in the collection

    Argues that death is a harm because it deprives a person of the good they would have had.

  14. 1992
    Peter Carruthersearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Animals Issue (1992) he maintains that animals have no direct moral status, since they cannot take part in a moral contract; our obligations toward them would be only indirect.

  15. 1992
    Luc Ferryearliest work cited in the collection

    In The New Ecological Order (1992) he criticizes deep ecology and antispeciesism, defending a humanism in which only humans have full dignity.

  16. 1992
    Eric Katzearliest work cited in the collection

    Criticizes restoration of and intervention in nature as forms of human domination over it.

  17. 1992
    Eugene Hargroveearliest work cited in the collection

    Grounds the value of nature in its aesthetic qualities.

  18. 1992
    Tyler Cowenearliest work cited in the collection

    In Policing Nature (2003) he argues that, in principle, we have reasons to reduce predation when it is feasible.

  19. 1992
    Jürgen Habermasearliest work cited in the collection

    Formulated discourse ethics: valid norms are those that everyone affected would accept in a free dialogue.

  20. 1993
    Mark Sagoffearliest work cited in the collection

    In Animal Liberation and Environmental Ethics: Bad Marriage, Quick Divorce (1984) he argues that animal advocacy and environmentalism are incompatible.

  21. 1993
    Ingmar Perssonearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends a basis for equality between species (A Basis for (Interspecies) Equality, 1993).

  22. 1993
    Larry Temkinearliest work cited in the collection

    A theorist of egalitarianism and of how to compare outcomes (Inequality, 1993; Rethinking the Good, 2012).

  23. 1994
    Ned Hettingerearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends the value of "wild" nature and discusses exotic species and respect for the autonomy of natural processes.

  24. 1994
    Carol J. Adamsearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Sexual Politics of Meat (1990) she links the oppression of women to that of animals; she is associated with the ethics of care.

  25. 1994
    David Sobelearliest work cited in the collection

    Works on theories of well-being based on informed desires.

  26. 1995
    Gary L. Francioneearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends the abolitionist approach: the central problem is that animals are property; he proposes veganism as a moral baseline and criticizes welfarist reforms.

  27. 1995
    Evelyn Pluharearliest work cited in the collection

    In Beyond Prejudice (1995) she develops the argument from "marginal cases": if humans without certain capacities count, animals without them count too.

  28. 1995
    Fishcountearliest work cited in the collection

    Estimates the number of fish caught and farmed each year worldwide.

  29. 1996
    Josephine Donovanearliest work cited in the collection

    Develops the ethics of care as applied to animals, centered on empathy and attention to suffering.

  30. 1996
    Yves Bonnardelearliest work cited in the collection

    Criticizes the idealization of nature and argues that antispeciesism must also take wild animals into account.

  31. 1997
    Gail Eisnitzearliest work cited in the collection

    In Slaughterhouse (1997) she gathered testimony from slaughterhouse workers about what happens to the animals.

  32. 1997
    Hugh LaFolletteearliest work cited in the collection

    With Niall Shanks, in Brute Science (1996), he questions the scientific validity of animal experimentation.

  33. 1998
    Shelly Kaganearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Normative Ethics (1998), used in the collection to explain the "focal point" of theories. In How to Count Animals (2019) he defends a hierarchical view: animals matter, but less than persons.

  34. 1998
    Peter Wenzearliest work cited in the collection

    Proposes a "concentric circles" theory: stronger obligations toward those who are closer to us, within a pluralistic environmental ethics.

  35. 1998
    Mark Rowlandsearliest work cited in the collection

    Applies Rawls's contractualism to animals: behind the veil of ignorance we would not know whether we would be human, so justice would include animals.

  36. 1998
    David Wassermanearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of the entry on the concept of discrimination in the Encyclopedia of Applied Ethics.

  37. 1999
    Lewis Petrinovichearliest work cited in the collection

    In Darwinian Dominion (1999) he argues that favoring one's own species is natural and morally justified by evolution.

The 2000s

  1. 2000
    Richard D. Ryderearliest work cited in the collection

    Coined the term "speciesism" in 1970 and later proposed "painism": what matters is each individual's pain.

  2. 2001
    Paola Cavalieriearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Animal Question (2001) she extends human rights theory to animals; with Singer, she organized the Great Ape Project.

  3. 2001
    Jennifer Everettearliest work cited in the collection

    Discusses the "predation problem" at the intersection of environmental ethics and animal ethics.

  4. 2001
    Jennifer Matherearliest work cited in the collection

    Researches cognition and sentience in invertebrates, especially octopuses.

  5. 2002
    Jeff McMahanearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Ethics of Killing (2002) he proposes that the harm of death depends on "time-relative interests" and on psychological connectedness with the future, which would make death less serious for many animals. He has also written about the moral problem of predation.

  6. 2002
    Jessica Ehnertearliest work cited in the collection

    Systematized the argument from species overlap (The Argument from Species Overlap, 2002).

  7. 2002
    Nathan Nobisearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends moral arguments for veganism and against the use of animals.

  8. 2003
    Bernice Bovenkerkearliest work cited in the collection

    Works at the intersection of animal ethics and environmental ethics, including the management of wild animals.

  9. 2003
    Nick Bostromearliest work cited in the collection

    Popularized the concept of existential risk and has written about artificial superintelligence.

  10. 2003
    Raimond Gaitaearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Philosopher's Dog (2002) he explores the relationship between humans and animals, defending a view that sets apart the moral place of humans.

  11. 2003
    Donald Broomearliest work cited in the collection

    Studies indicators of sentience and of well-being in many species.

  12. 2004
    Jo-Ann Sheltonearliest work cited in the collection

    Analyzes environmentalist programs that kill animals considered "out of place," such as introduced species.

  13. 2004
    Lynne Sneddonearliest work cited in the collection

    A pioneering researcher on pain in fish.

  14. 2005
    Christine Korsgaardearliest work cited in the collection

    In Fellow Creatures (2018) she argues, starting from Kant, that animals are also ends in themselves, against the reading that only indirect duties would apply to them.

  15. 2005
    Scott Wilsonearliest work cited in the collection

    Argues that human status depends on the "species norm" (The Species-Norm Account of Human Status, 2005).

  16. 2005
    EFSAearliest work cited in the collection

    Has published scientific opinions on the sentience and welfare of animals used in production.

  17. 2006
    Rosalind Hursthouseearliest work cited in the collection

    Argues, from virtue ethics, that cruelty and indifference toward animals are vices.

  18. 2006
    Martha Nussbaumearliest work cited in the collection

    Extends the capabilities approach to animals: each should be able to flourish according to its own form of life (Justice for Animals, 2023).

  19. 2006
    Gaverick Mathenyearliest work cited in the collection

    Defended utilitarianism applied to animals and was a pioneer in proposing cultivated meat as a way to reduce suffering.

  20. 2007
    Oscar Hortaearliest work cited in the collection

    One of the leading authors on speciesism and on the suffering of wild animals; argues that natural harms matter just as much as those caused by humans.

  21. 2007
    Carlos Naconecyearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Ética & Animais (2006), an introduction to the debate in Portuguese.

  22. 2007
    Brian Tomasikearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Essays on Reducing Suffering; estimates the numbers of animals (especially insects) and advocates reducing the suffering of wild animals and the risks of future suffering.

  23. 2007
    Nils Holtugearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends prioritarianism (benefits are worth more to those who are worse off) and has written about the harm of death.

  24. 2008
    David DeGraziaearliest work cited in the collection

    In Taking Animals Seriously (1996) he defends serious consideration for animals, but allows different weights depending on mental complexity, especially regarding the harm of death.

  25. 2009
    Aaron Simmonsearliest work cited in the collection

    Discusses animal rights in the face of predation and the duty of assistance.

  26. 2009
    David Pearceearliest work cited in the collection

    In The Hedonistic Imperative (1995) he proposes using biotechnology to abolish the suffering of all sentient beings, including in nature.

2010 onward

  1. 2011
    Catia Fariaearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Animal Ethics in the Wild (2023); argues that we have reasons to help wild animals harmed by natural processes.

  2. 2012
    Richard Posnerearliest work cited in the collection

    In a debate with Singer, he argued that it is legitimate to give preference to humans and to trust the intuition that humans matter more.

  3. 2013
    Marc Bekoffearliest work cited in the collection

    Advocates "compassionate conservation," which tries to reconcile conservation with the good of each animal.

  4. 2013
    Nick Becksteadearliest work cited in the collection

    In his dissertation (2013) he argued for the enormous importance of shaping the distant future.

  5. 2014
    Cheryl Abbateearliest work cited in the collection

    Applies virtue ethics to the animal question, showing that it too condemns harming animals unnecessarily.

  6. 2015
    Adriano Manninoearliest work cited in the collection

    Defends suffering-focused ethics and concern for wild animals and for risks of future suffering.

  7. 2015
    Daniel Doradoearliest work cited in the collection

    Writes about the ethics of intervening in nature on behalf of animals.

  8. 2015
    Lukas Gloorearliest work cited in the collection

    Has written about s-risks and about the asymmetry between suffering and happiness.

  9. 2016
    Corey Lee Wrennearliest work cited in the collection

    Studies the animal advocacy movement and defends an abolitionist and intersectional approach.

  10. 2016
    Guy Fletcherearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of an introduction to theories of well-being (2016).

  11. 2016
    Andrew Barronearliest work cited in the collection

    With Colin Klein, he argued that insects have brain structures capable of subjective experience (2016).

  12. 2017
    Tobias Baumannearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Avoiding the Worst (2022); the collection's main reference on s-risks (risks of suffering on an astronomical scale) and how to prevent them.

  13. 2017
    Jacy Reese Anthisearliest work cited in the collection

    In The End of Animal Farming (2018) he analyzes strategies for ending animal agriculture, including food technology and institutional change.

  14. 2018
    Luciano Carlos Cunhaearliest work cited in the collection

    In the works cited, he defends equal consideration for all sentient beings, including wild animals who are victims of natural processes.

  15. 2019
    Yew-Kwang Ngearliest work cited in the collection

    In Towards Welfare Biology (1995) he proposed "welfare biology" and argued that suffering prevails in nature because of mass reproduction.

  16. 2019
    Magnus Vindingearliest work cited in the collection

    Author of Suffering-Focused Ethics (2020); argues for prioritizing the reduction of intense suffering, including in the future.

  17. 2019
    William MacAskillearliest work cited in the collection

    In What We Owe the Future (2022) he defends longtermism: giving great weight to how our decisions affect the distant future.

  18. 2019
    Hilary Greavesearliest work cited in the collection

    Coauthor, with MacAskill, of The Case for Strong Longtermism.

  19. 2020
    Kyle Johannsenearliest work cited in the collection

    In Wild Animal Ethics (2021) he advocates research-based interventions to reduce the suffering of wild animals.

  20. 2021
    Asher Sorylearliest work cited in the collection

    Works on the well-being of wild animals and on the feasibility of interventions to help them.

  21. 2021
    Jonathan Birchearliest work cited in the collection

    Led the report that led the United Kingdom to recognize the sentience of octopuses, squid, crabs and lobsters (2021); defends the precautionary principle.
