Does It Matter What We Would Do on a Desert Island or in a Burning House?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. Extreme scenarios
  2. Problem 1: what we'd do isn't always what's right
  3. Problem 2: extremes apply to humans too
  4. Problem 3: an emergency isn't everyday life
  5. In short

Extreme scenarios

It's common to hear questions like these:

  • "If you were on a desert island and only had an animal to eat, wouldn't you eat it?"
  • "If the house were on fire and you could only save a baby or a dog, who would you save?"

The idea is to show that, deep down, everyone would favor humans, so eating animals in everyday life would be justified.

Problem 1: what we'd do isn't always what's right

The argument assumes that what most people would do in a situation like that is automatically right. But when they're desperate, people do lots of things that aren't right.

Problem 2: extremes apply to humans too

In extreme situations, there are reports of people doing terrible things to other humans to survive. Nobody uses that to say those things are acceptable in everyday life. The same goes for animals.

Problem 3: an emergency isn't everyday life

This is the main problem. Whatever the right choice is in a disaster, you can't carry that choice over to a completely different situation.

On the desert island, the issue would be survival. At the grocery store, nobody's life is in danger: there are lots of other food options. Comparing the two is like saying that because it's okay to break a window to escape a fire, it would be okay to break windows for fun.

New word

Hypothetical scenario: an imagined situation used to test an idea.

Remember

What we do in an emergency doesn't decide what's right to do when we have a choice.

In short

  • What most people would do when desperate isn't necessarily right.
  • In extreme situations, people also do things to humans that nobody approves of in everyday life.
  • Emergencies don't justify what we do when we have other options.
