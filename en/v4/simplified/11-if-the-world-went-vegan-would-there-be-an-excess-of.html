Simplified version · Volume IV · chapter 11

If the World Went Vegan, Would There Be an Excess of Domesticated Animals?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. The objection
  2. Problem 1: even then, there'd be less suffering
  3. Problem 2: the opposite would happen
  4. It wouldn't happen all at once
  5. In short

The objection

"If everyone went vegan, the world would be overrun with cows, pigs, and chickens, because nobody would kill them anymore."

Problem 1: even then, there'd be less suffering

Even if that happened, such a world would probably have less suffering than today's, where tens of trillions of animals are killed every year. And in a vegan world, people would care about animals and look for ways to take care of them.

Problem 2: the opposite would happen

This is the main problem. Today there are billions of cows, pigs, and chickens because humans make them be born, through controlled breeding on farms. These animals aren't "left over" in nature: they're produced.

If people stopped buying animal products, farmers would stop breeding the animals. The number of farmed animals would drop, little by little, as more people went vegan.

It wouldn't happen all at once

The world doesn't go vegan overnight. The change would be gradual. Each year, fewer animals would be born for exploitation.

New word

Gradual: happening little by little, step by step.

Remember

There are only so many farmed animals today because of exploitation. Without it, fewer would be born.

In short

  • Even a world with more animals would have less suffering than today's.
  • Farmed animals exist because humans make them be born.
  • In a vegan world, there would be fewer farmed animals, not more.
