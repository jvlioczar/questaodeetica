The Total Approach and the Sufficient Approach

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

  3. Ideas that fit with many views

How do we convince more people?

There are two approaches to getting more people to accept the idea of a better world for all beings that can feel.

Compatibility ranges

Some ideas can only be accepted along with lots of other ideas. Other ideas fit with many different views. This is called a compatibility range.

New word

Compatibility range: how many other ideas someone has to accept in order to accept a certain idea. A wide range fits with many different views.

If we think an idea requires accepting lots of others, but it actually doesn't, we miss the chance to convince many people.

Ideas that fit with many views

To accept the goals below, you don't need to follow any particular ethical theory or belong to any particular political camp:

  • consideration for all sentient beings;
  • rejecting speciesism;
  • ending animal exploitation, and veganism;
  • reducing the suffering of wild animals;
  • preventing risks of suffering in the future.

Two approaches

  • Full approach: standing up for what we think is the best decision, all of it.
  • Sufficient approach: showing that even people who disagree with part of our position can accept some of it, or some of its consequences.

For example: someone might not accept full veganism, but accept that animals matter and cut back a lot on what they consume.

Use both

Each approach on its own has risks. Only defending the ideal can push away people who aren't ready yet. Only asking for the minimum can lead people to stop halfway. The best option seems to be to combine the two.

Remember

Standing up for the ideal while also showing that every step counts can bring more people to the cause.

In short

  • Many goals of the animal cause fit with different ethical and political views.
  • The full approach defends the ideal; the sufficient approach shows that every step counts.
  • It's best to use both together.
