Simplified version · Volume VIII · chapter 4

The Use of Anthropocentric and Environmentalist Arguments in the Animal Cause

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. A common strategy
  2. Different reasons, different results
  3. The risks
  4. In short

A common strategy

Many animal advocates try to convince people with arguments that aren't about the animals. For example:

  • "animal agriculture is bad for the environment";
  • "meat is bad for your health";
  • "eating animals isn't natural";
  • "mistreating animals trains people to mistreat people";
  • "the way meat is produced is disgusting."

The idea is: "For the animals, what matters is that people stop exploiting them, not why." And: "Since people are speciesist, these arguments are more convincing."

Different reasons, different results

The author shows that the reason does matter:

  • Someone who stops eating meat for their health might start again if a "healthy" meat comes along.
  • Someone who stops for the environment might accept "sustainable" exploitation, or even support killing wild animals in the name of nature.
  • Only someone who stops because animals matter has a reason that holds in every situation.

The risks

  • Suggesting that animals don't matter: if the problem is only health or the environment, then exploiting animals would be fine whenever it didn't harm humans.
  • Strengthening ideas that work against animals: anthropocentrism and environmentalism hold values that clash with consideration for animals.
  • Increasing exploitation: for example, switching from beef to chicken or fish "for health" leads to many more animals dying, because they're smaller animals.
  • Forgetting wild animals: environmentalist arguments can lead people to reject helping them.

New word

Indirect strategy: trying to reach a goal by using arguments that aren't directly about it.

Remember

For animals, it matters not only what people do, but why they do it. The right reasons go further.

In short

  • Health or environmental arguments seem to convince people faster.
  • But different reasons lead to different results.
  • Relying only on these arguments can even increase animal suffering.
