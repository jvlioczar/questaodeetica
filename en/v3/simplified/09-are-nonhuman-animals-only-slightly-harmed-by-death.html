Simplified version · Volume III · chapter 9

Are Nonhuman Animals Only Slightly Harmed by Death?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 3 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. The idea being debated
  2. The "prudential reasons" argument
  3. Example 1: the bigger harm
  4. Example 2: the bigger good
  5. The conclusion
  6. In short

The idea being debated

We've already seen the idea that animals would be only slightly harmed by death because they have little psychological connectedness with their future. Chapter 7 showed they can have strong connectedness. Now the question is different: why would connectedness matter?

The "prudential reasons" argument

Some say we only have strong reasons to care about our future to the extent that we're connected to it.

New word

Prudential reasons: reasons to do something for your own good, like saving money for the future.

There are two examples that show this doesn't work.

Example 1: the bigger harm

Anna knows that late in life she'll have Alzheimer's, and the disease will erase every link to who she is today. She has to choose:

  • feel a smaller pain now, while she's still "herself";
  • or feel a bigger pain in the future, when she no longer has any connection to who she is now.

From Anna's own point of view, choosing the smaller pain seems better, even though it's now. In other words, the bigger pain in the future still matters a lot, even without psychological connectedness.

Example 2: the bigger good

Bella is five and dreams of seeing the pyramids of Egypt. She can travel now and enjoy it a little, or go when she's an adult and enjoy it much more, even though she'll be very different from the Bella of today.

It seems Bella has good reasons to prefer the more enjoyable trip, in the future. The size of the good counts more than psychological connectedness.

The conclusion

What matters is how much good or bad someone will live through, not how similar that person stays to themselves over time. So even if an animal had little psychological connectedness, losing its future would still be a big harm for it.

Remember

An animal's death is serious because it takes away the good the animal would have lived, no matter how much it "thinks about the future."

In short

  • The examples of Anna and Bella show that the size of the good and bad counts more than connectedness.
  • So the idea that animals lose little by dying doesn't hold up.
  • Losing the future is a big harm for any being that would have had a good life.
