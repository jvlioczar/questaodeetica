Objections to the Account of the Harm of Death as the Prevention of What Is Positive

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. The explanation we're testing
  2. Objection 1: "you can't know the future"
  3. Objection 2: "then why don't we regret not being born earlier?"

The explanation we're testing

In chapter 1 we saw that death is bad because it takes away the good things that were still to come. There are three objections to this explanation and the answers to them.

Objection 1: "you can't know the future"

We're never sure whether someone's life would be good or bad from here on. So how can we know if death would be a harm?

Answer: we don't need certainty. We can make estimates. A doctor doesn't know exactly how long a patient will live, but knows that treating a disease is usually worth it. Death is similar: we can judge what's most likely.

New word

Epistemological problem: a difficulty about what we can know, not about what is true.

Objection 2: "then why don't we regret not being born earlier?"

If losing time alive in the future is bad, then losing time alive in the past (by being born later) should be bad too. But nobody regrets not being born earlier.

Answer: in real life, nobody can be born earlier. But the moment of death can change. That's why it makes sense to care about it. Also, if it were possible to choose, it would make sense to prefer having more time alive, whether it comes before or after.

Objection 3: "what about beings who were never born?"

If death is bad because it prevents good things, then someone who was never born would also have been harmed by not living. That seems strange.

Answer: it only makes sense to talk about harm for someone who already exists. Someone who never existed isn't someone who lost something. This answer also raises hard questions, for example about whether it's good for new happy beings to be born.

Remember

The objections show the subject is complex, but they don't knock down the main idea: dying is bad because it takes away the good that was coming.

In short

  • Even without certainty, we can estimate whether a life would be good.
  • The moment of death can change; the moment of birth can't.
  • Only someone who already exists can lose something by dying.
