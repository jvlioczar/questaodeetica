Do Species Have Interests?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. What it means to have interests
  2. The idea being debated
  3. The answers
  4. The word "interest" is misleading
  5. In short

What it means to have interests

New word

Interest: to have an interest in something is to benefit if it happens, or be harmed if it doesn't.

In this sense, saying we should consider sentient beings "because they have interests" is the same as saying they can be harmed or helped.

The idea being debated

Some writers say species have interests too, because they:

  • carry out complex activities that would be more than the sum of what their members do;
  • have a past, a present, and a "direction toward the future";
  • have their own unity and identity;
  • would have a stronger interest in continuing to exist than their members, because they don't have to die.

This could be used to justify killing animals to preserve species.

The answers

  • A species' complex activities can be explained by what its members do, without assuming anything extra.
  • Having a past, a present, unity, and identity isn't enough to have interests. Political parties and cities have all of that too, and nobody thinks a city can suffer.
  • To really have interests, things have to be able to be good or bad for you. And that requires feeling. A species doesn't feel anything.

The word "interest" is misleading

Sometimes we say "it's in the species' interest" as a figure of speech, just as we say "the car needs oil." The car doesn't suffer without oil; it just stops working.

Remember

Only those who feel can really have interests. Species don't feel, so they don't have interests of their own.

In short

  • Having interests means being able to be helped or harmed.
  • The arguments that species have interests would apply to cities too.
  • Only beings who feel really have interests.
