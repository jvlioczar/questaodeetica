Before you read study layer, not part of the original text

Helping wild animals already happens: there is vaccination (for example, against rabies), treatment of diseases (probiotics against mange and white-nose syndrome), rescues during disasters, and care for orphans; much more could be done.

There are already people who vaccinate wild animals, treat diseases in bats and frogs, and rescue animals from fires. So helping is not a dream: it already happens, and much more can be done.

Anyone who doubts that flying is possible only needs to look at an airplane. Anyone who doubts that we can help wild animals only needs to look at the programs that already exist.

  1. P1 The objection says the proposal is impractical.
  2. P2 Wild animals are already vaccinated, treated for diseases, and rescued from situations of need.
  3. P3 Some treatments have greatly increased survival, as in the case of bats with white-nose syndrome.
  4. C So the proposal is not impractical, and much more could be done.

The chapter’s central argument in logical form. If the premises are true, the conclusion follows. To disagree, you have to show which premise is false.

They say “It is impossible to help wild animals.”

Answer It is already being done: rabies vaccination for foxes, probiotics for bats and frogs, rescues from fires. The biggest obstacle is not technical; it is a matter of priorities.

26.1. Introduction

Some people believe that the proposal to help , despite having good theoretical foundations, is impracticable. The way to respond to this is simply to show what is already being done to help animals in the wild.

Various things are already being done to help animals in the wild. These aid programs normally have or goals. However, the animals benefit too, and this by itself already shows that it is false that there is nothing that can be done to help them. Moreover, the knowledge gained from these programs can be useful in guiding future projects whose goal is to help animals.

Among the ways in which animals are already being helped80, we can highlight vaccination and disease treatment, helping animals in fires and other natural disasters, caring for orphaned animals, rescuing trapped animals and programs to meet animals' basic needs. Below are some examples of these forms of help.

26.2. Vaccination

There are many examples of large-scale vaccination of wild animals. The best known is rabies vaccination, which has taken place in several countries and helped eradicate the disease in large parts of Europe and North America81. Vaccination is carried out by aerial dispersal of baits containing the vaccine82. The use of drones makes it possible to vaccinate 4,000 animals per day83. Similar programs have been implemented around the world, including the vaccination of wild dogs in Africa84 and Asia85, and of wolves in Ethiopia86.

The following examples show the effectiveness of this type of project. Sylvatic plague in prairie dogs has mortality rates close to 100%87, but after a vaccination campaign in South Dakota in 2019, the animals showed a survival rate of 95%88. Anthrax vaccination has proven effective in populations of rhinoceroses, zebras and cheetahs89, and has also been proposed for gorillas90. Wild boars were vaccinated against tuberculosis under natural transmission conditions in 201191. Research at the University of Helsinki found that it is possible to vaccinate thousands of bees simply by vaccinating the queen92. In the United Kingdom there is a Vaccine and Antigen Bank93. Through vaccination, rinderpest became the second disease ever to be completely eradicated (the first was smallpox) and the first among those that affect nonhuman animals94.

26.3. Disease treatment

Probiotics have been used to treat wombats for mange95; fish for various types of infections96; bats for white-nose syndrome (which increased survival rates from 8% to 46%97) and boreal toads for skin diseases (which gave them a survival rate 40% higher than that of other toads98). There is also research on probiotic treatments for snakes99 and bees100. Since the 1940s, insect sterilization has been used to halt the spread of diseases, which benefits both the animals who would be bitten and the insects themselves since, given their population dynamics101, of all those born, few manage to survive102.

26.4. Treating animals in need

Animals have also been helped when they are injured. For example, birds and bats brought to rehabilitation centers with broken wings usually make a full recovery103. Another form of help is the rescue of trapped animals, such as whales trapped in ice104, elephants in mud lakes105 and animals stranded on beaches106. Providing food combined with contraceptives has also had good results107.

There are orphanages for wild animals in South Africa, Kenya and the Republic of the Congo108. Baby turtles have also been helped to reach the water after hatching109. Animals have also been rescued in disasters such as fires110, hurricanes and tsunamis111, volcanic eruptions112 and floods113.

Sometimes animals are also helped through the construction of shelters or nests114, which has benefited bats115, pigeons116, rabbits117 and moths118. The construction of artificial highlands in Kaziranga National Park, in India, cut the number of deaths of large animals in floods by half in two years119.

These are just a few examples of what is already being done to help animals in the wild. However, much more could be done. As we will see in the next chapter, one example would be the establishment of the field of 120, which would study animals in their ecosystems from the standpoint of what affects them as individuals who have well-being (and not as specimens of species and components of ecosystems).
