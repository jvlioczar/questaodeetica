How Not to Understand the Issue

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. A common mistake
  2. Mistake 1: the contradiction

A common mistake

Many people say: "What happens in nature, like hunger, disease, and predation, isn't an ethical issue, because nobody is to blame. Not nature, and not the animals."

There are two mistakes in this idea.

Mistake 1: the contradiction

Many people say both of these things at once:

  • "this isn't an ethical issue";
  • "we have a duty not to help these animals."

But saying we have a duty not to help is already an ethical claim! The two statements contradict each other.

And even someone who says helping would be optional is doing ethics, because ethics is about not only what is required and forbidden, but also what is allowed.

New word

Ethical issue: any question about what we should do, may do, or shouldn't do.

Mistake 2: blame isn't what matters

Nobody has to be to blame for suffering to be bad. When a flood hits a town, nobody is to blame for the rain. Still, helping the victims is clearly an ethical issue.

Ethics doesn't only ask "whose fault is it?" It also asks "what should we do about it?"

Remember

Whenever we can ask whether we should or shouldn't do something, we're facing an ethical issue.

In short

  • Saying "we shouldn't help" is already an ethical position.
  • Ethics deals with what is required, forbidden, and allowed.
  • Nobody has to be to blame for suffering to deserve help.
