Simplified version · Volume VII · chapter 9

Is the Proposal to Help Wild Animals Welfarist?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. The accusation
  2. The word has several meanings
  3. Where the confusion is
  4. In short

The accusation

"Helping wild animals is welfarism." For many animal advocates, this word is a criticism, because it suggests just "improving" exploitation instead of ending it.

The word has several meanings

The author shows that "welfarism" can mean three different things:

  • 1. Thinking there's nothing wrong with exploiting animals, as long as their suffering is reduced.
  • 2. Thinking exploitation is unjust and should end, but that regulating it can lead to its end.
  • 3. Thinking exploitation is unjust and should end, but that, in the meantime, regulating it reduces animals' suffering.

There's a debate about whether regulations really lead to the end of exploitation or reduce suffering. To avoid confusion, the author doesn't even use the word "welfarist" for these positions.

Where the confusion is

All three of these positions are about animal exploitation. The proposal to help wild animals has nothing to do with exploitation. It aims to prevent natural harms, like disease and hunger, that humans don't cause.

So calling it "welfarist" mixes up different topics. Wanting animals to be well isn't the same as accepting exploitation.

New word

Abolitionism: the position that animal exploitation should end completely, not just be made less bad.

Remember

Standing up for the well-being of wild animals isn't accepting exploitation. It's wanting them to suffer less.

In short

  • "Welfarism" has several meanings, all tied to exploitation.
  • Helping wild animals has nothing to do with exploitation.
  • So the accusation mixes up different topics.
