Simplified version · Volume VII · chapter 32

Discussing Our Ability to Assess When Life Is or Is Not Worth Living

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. The starting point
  2. The objection
  3. It's hard in some cases
  4. But most cases are extreme
  5. "But life could get better"
  6. In short

The starting point

As we've seen, the vast majority of animals born in the wild experience mostly suffering and die very young. For many of them, it seems life wasn't worth living.

The objection

"We're not in a position to judge whether an animal's life is worth living. A life that looks bad from the outside might be good for the one living it."

It's hard in some cases

It's true that there are hard cases, where we can't tell whether a life is worth living.

But most cases are extreme

Almost all the cases the proposal deals with are extreme situations: a baby animal that is born and starves within a few days, a tadpole eaten right after hatching, an animal slowly dying of a disease. In cases like these, it's really hard to imagine that life was good for the one who lived it.

"But life could get better"

Someone might say: "A life might have been bad so far but get better later." That's true. But it's not an objection to the proposal to help. Quite the opposite: it's exactly a reason to help, so that these animals' lives get better.

New word

Life worth living: a life that, overall, has more good things than bad things for the one living it.

Remember

In many cases, we don't need absolute certainty to see that a short life of intense suffering was bad for the one who lived it.

In short

  • Some cases are hard to judge, but most are extreme.
  • Starving to death right after birth is hardly a good life.
  • If a life can get better, that's one more reason to help.
