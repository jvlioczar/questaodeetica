What Technologies Could Be Used to Help Animals in the Wild?

Before you read study layer, not part of the original text

Technologies such as individual identification of insects, population modeling, thermal imaging, drones, and data analysis can help us understand and improve the well-being of animals in the wild, and their use will expand as awareness grows.

Cameras that see animals' body heat, drones, and computers that analyze lots of data can help us find out where animals are suffering and how to help them. Technology can be on the animals' side.

Just as medical imaging changed medicine, new technologies can change how much we know about the well-being of wild animals.

  1. P1 New technologies make it possible to observe and measure things in the lives of wild animals that were impossible before.
  2. P2 This data can show what affects them positively or negatively.
  3. P3 With more awareness of the situation of wild animals, this use can expand.
  4. C So technology can help prevent and reduce natural harms.

The chapter’s central argument in logical form. If the premises are true, the conclusion follows. To disagree, you have to show which premise is false.

They say “Even if we wanted to, we would have no way of knowing what happens to each animal.”

Answer Thermal imaging, sensors, drones, and data analysis already make it possible to estimate populations and identify individuals. Our knowledge is likely to grow.

Original text Luciano Carlos Cunha · Volume VII, p. 83 of the PDF edition · English translation

New technologies allow us to observe things that were never possible before, such as identifying insects individually and recognizing them later, and modeling how the various animal populations in a habitat affect one another's presence and numbers. Analysis of the data collected can inform us about the presence of species in an area, their numbers, the way the distribution of different groups of animals changes over time, and the factors that contribute to this, such as the animals' mortality, health and behavior. We will look at some examples below133.

Camera traps are cameras with motion sensors that are installed in the natural habitat. They can take photos periodically or record videos at specific times, providing continuous updates. They can collect information about the location and number of animals, their behaviors and physical condition, helping to monitor the animals' well-being and the factors that affect it, such as rapidly growing populations, the presence of other species, resource limitations in the area and the way the animals use those resources.

Light detection and ranging (LIDAR) technology, also known as 3D laser scanning, produces three-dimensional images of landscapes. It can be deployed on the ground or on an aerial vehicle and produces high-resolution images that show the structure of a habitat in detail, down to the position of leaves on branches. It can detect the abundance and distribution of species populations as well as the physical properties of the habitat that affect those populations. The data can help us understand animals' resource needs and how adequately they are being met.

Thermal imaging134 uses infrared cameras to capture the heat signatures of animals in an area. It can determine the presence of particular kinds of animals and their population numbers, including those of camouflaged animals. One of its greatest uses is detecting physical stress, which may occur because of an injury, disease, fear or some other kind of threat response. It also makes it possible to identify localized injuries.

Acoustic data analysis (of the sounds of animals and of the environments in which they live) provides information about health, interactions between animals and changes in a habitat's conditions. Recordings include infrasonic and ultrasonic sounds, both beyond the range of human hearing. There are microphones that work in water as well as on land and in the air.

Environmental DNA (eDNA)135 is a way of collecting genetic material in natural environments, such as hair, feathers, feces and scales. It requires no interaction with the animals, thus avoiding any negative impact on them. It provides information about an animal's diet, health and age. It can estimate the kinds of animals living in an area and their numbers.

Continuous improvements in data processing power will increase our ability to use data to gain insights into animals' well-being. As awareness of the situation of grows, the use of these technologies will expand, providing much more practical information on how to help animals.
