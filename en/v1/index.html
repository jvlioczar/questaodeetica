Volume I · Basic level

Discussing Speciesism

An analysis of the main attempts to justify speciesism

15 chaptersabout 58 min of reading52 notes78 references

0 of 15 read

About this volume

This book offers a critical analysis of the arguments most commonly advanced in an attempt to defend the view that nonhuman animals either should not receive moral consideration or should receive it to a lesser degree than humans.

ISBN 978-65-01-30510-3 · 1st edition, January 2025. Original text by Luciano Carlos Cunha, translated into English.

In a nutshell

Why do we treat a pig and a dog, or an animal and a human, so differently? This volume examines, one by one, the most common justifications for giving animals less weight (species, soul, intelligence, nature, tradition, authority, metaethics) and shows why none of them holds up.

For kids

Imagine someone says only kids with long hair can play on the playground. That is unfair, because hair has nothing to do with playing. This book shows that treating an animal badly just because it belongs to another species is similar: species has nothing to do with being able to feel pain.

You will learn

  • What speciesism is and how it differs from anthropocentrism
  • Why species, soul, intelligence and potential do not justify disregarding animals
  • Why "it's natural," "it's tradition" and "an authority approved it" are not good reasons
  • How to respond when someone says ethics is just a human invention

It helps to have read: nothing. This is a good starting point, together with Volume XII.

Chapters

  1. 1
    An Overview of the Debate on the Moral Consideration of Animals

    There are three main positions on animals: they don't matter in themselves, they matter less than humans, or they matter equally when they suffer harms of the same size. Speciesism is giving someone less weight just because of their species.

    6 min
  2. 2
    Is Species What Matters?

    Saying humans matter more “because they're human,” or “because they're our species,” justifies nothing: it just repeats the conclusion as if it were the reason.

    3 min
  3. 3
    Could Metaphysical Characteristics Justify Speciesism?

    An immortal soul, being made in the image of a deity, or other metaphysical traits don't justify speciesism: they can't be proven, they wouldn't apply only to humans, and above all they have nothing to do with being able to suffer.

    2 min
  4. 4
    Could the Possession of Capacities or Relationships Justify Speciesism?

    Reason, language, a sense of justice, or political relationships don't justify disregarding animals: many humans lack them, some animals have some of them, and they're irrelevant to who can be harmed.

    2 min
  5. 5
    Could Humans' Supposed Potential Justify Speciesism?

    The appeal to potential fails because some humans have no potential to develop certain abilities, because potential isn't the same as having, and because a child's suffering matters now, not because of what she might become.

    2 min
  6. 6
    Is What Matters Belonging to the Same Species as Those Who Have Certain Capacities?

    Saying what matters is belonging to the same species as those who have certain abilities is biased: it demands the abilities from animals but accepts humans without them just because of their group.

    5 min
  7. 7
    If Species Are Not a Social Construct, Is Speciesism Justified?

    Even if species is a biological category and not a social construction, that doesn't make it fair to discriminate by species: enslaving people by height or skin color would also be based on biological traits, and it would be unfair.

    2 min
  8. 8
    If Speciesism Is Natural, Is Speciesism Justified?

    From the fact that something is natural, common among animals, or instinctive, it doesn't follow that it's good or fair; there is no correlation between what is natural and what is justifiable.

    4 min
  9. 9
    Does the Fact That Speciesist Practices Are Traditions Show That We Should Respect Them?

    A practice being traditional doesn't show that it's fair: many traditions have been unjust, and defending traditions because they benefit people or because they supposedly have intrinsic value doesn't justify harming the victims.

    5 min
  10. 10
    If Ethics Is a Human Activity, Is It Just to Disregard Animals?

    That only humans act morally doesn't show that only humans should be considered: confusing who can be held responsible with who needs consideration is like requiring a patient to be a doctor in order to be treated.

    3 min
  11. 11
    If Ethics Is an Invention, Is It Just to Disadvantage Animals?

    Even if ethics is a human invention, that doesn't make all positions equally arbitrary: math and science are also created activities, and in them some positions are better and some worse.

    3 min
  12. 12
    If Ethics Was Invented for Self-Protection, Is It Just to Disadvantage Animals?

    Even if ethics arose as self-protection, describing how an activity arose doesn't justify a view within it; and the idea that ethics is just self-interest doesn't even describe well how people act.

    3 min
  13. 13
    Attempts to Justify Speciesism That Appeal to Authorities

    Appeals to authority, religious or otherwise, don't justify speciesism: by the Euthyphro dilemma, either the authority approves of something for good reasons, and then what matters is the reasons, or the approval is arbitrary.

    4 min
  14. 14
    The Appeal to Differentiated Degrees of Moral Status

    The special status thesis, according to which some have a “trump card” that gives them priority even when they suffer less, fails the impartiality test: only those in the privileged group defend it.

    8 min
  15. 15
    Attempts to Justify Speciesism That Appeal to Metaethics

    Saying “there's no right or wrong in ethics” doesn't justify speciesism: this metaethical claim usually shows up only when the justifications run out, it's applied with a double standard, and whether true or false, it favors neither side.

    6 min
