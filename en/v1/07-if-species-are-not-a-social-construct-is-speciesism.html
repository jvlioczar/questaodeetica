Before you read study layer, not part of the original text

Even if species is a biological category and not a social construction, that doesn't make it fair to discriminate by species: enslaving people by height or skin color would also be based on biological traits, and it would be unfair.

Having curly hair is something about your body, your biology. But that doesn't mean it's fair to treat people with curly hair badly. Being from another species is also something about the body, and it doesn't justify mistreatment either.

A rule that banned left-handed people from being treated at the hospital would be based on a real biological trait. It would still be an unfair rule.

  1. P1 Racism and sexism are unfair because they disadvantage individuals of certain groups in a biased way.
  2. P2 They would still be unfair even if race and gender were purely biological categories.
  3. P3 The same goes for species: being a biological category doesn't make it relevant to the weight of suffering.
  4. C Therefore, the distinction between biological and socially constructed doesn't justify speciesism.

The chapter’s central argument in logical form. If the premises are true, the conclusion follows. To disagree, you have to show which premise is false.

They say “Race is a social construct, species is biology. That's why speciesism isn't like racism.”

Answer What makes racism unfair isn't that race is a social construct, but the biased treatment. A biological criterion can be just as arbitrary as a social one.

7.1. The appeal to the distinction between the biological and the socially constructed

Nonhuman animals are exploited for the most diverse purposes. They are used for food, clothing, as test subjects, for entertainment and leisure, and as workers or tools. This use kills literally trillions of animals every year, usually after a life full of suffering. Animals living in the wild are also constantly victims of disease, hunger, thirst, natural disasters, and so on. In many of these cases, it would be possible to prevent their suffering and deaths. However, the standard view is that it is wrong to help them. It is commonly said that we should let nature take its course, even if the outcome is terrible for the animals.

In both kinds of situations, if the victims were human, such attitudes would be widely condemned. In fact, more than that: such attitudes would be considered monstrous, heinous. This moral double standard based on the victim's species has been accused of committing a form of discrimination analogous to racism and sexism: speciesism29

However, there are people who disagree with this comparison. For example, it is sometimes said that using the species criterion for questions of is not unjust because it is a criterion that refers to biological characteristics30. According to this argument, racism and sexism, on the other hand, would be unjust because the very ideas of race and gender are allegedly social constructs. Let us look at some problems with this argument.

7.2. Problems with the appeal to biological distinctions

A first problem is that it does not explain why a criterion's being based on biological characteristics or on a social construct would be relevant to whether it is just

or unjust. This is simply assumed by the argument, without any explanation.

A second problem is that there are examples of criteria referring to biological characteristics that are clearly unjust. Imagine, for example, a system of slavery based on individuals' height. If the fact that this criterion is based on a biological characteristic cannot make it just, then the fact that the species criterion is based on a biological characteristic cannot make it just either.

Indeed, it seems that a criterion's being based on a biological characteristic cannot even make it less unjust than it would be if it were based on a social construct. This is because the injustice of a criterion depends on its treating individuals in a biased way, and not on whether the criterion it uses refers to social constructs or to biological traits.

Racism and sexism are unjust because they disadvantage individuals of certain groups in a biased way. If it were shown, for example, that races and genders are not social constructs, racism and sexism would remain just as unjust as they would be if races and genders are social constructs. Yet the same is true in the case of . The fact that the concept of species refers to biological characteristics does not make speciesism just, nor does it even make it less unjust than other forms of discrimination, such as racism and sexism.

This chapter in Volume XII

How the author sums up this debate in The Essentials of Animal Ethics.

The species criterion is fair because it refers to biological characteristics (whereas the criteria of race and gender would be unfair because they are social constructions). (I, ch. 7).
The fact that a criterion is based on biological characteristics does not make it fair. For example, a system of slavery based on height or skin color would be based on biological characteristics. Yet it would be just as unjust as any other form of slavery. The same goes for slavery based on species.
