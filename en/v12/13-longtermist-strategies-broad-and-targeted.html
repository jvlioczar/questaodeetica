Longtermist Strategies: Broad and Targeted

Before you read study layer, not part of the original text

Longtermist strategies can be broad (a good impact in almost every scenario, such as increasing consideration for sentient beings) or targeted (a large impact in specific scenarios, such as regulating space exploration or aligning AI); in choosing them, we need humility and widely acceptable goals.

To take care of the future, we can do things that help in almost any situation, like teaching respect, or things that help a lot in one specific situation, like rules for robots. The best thing is to do both, carefully.

Saving money helps with almost any emergency; buying car insurance helps a lot with one specific kind of emergency.

  1. P1 Broad strategies have a positive impact in a great many scenarios, but a smaller one in each.
  2. P2 Targeted strategies can have a large impact, but only in the scenario they address.
  3. P3 Biases and overconfidence can distort the choice.
  4. C So we need to combine strategies, cultivate humility, and advocate goals that many can accept.

The chapter’s central argument in logical form. If the premises are true, the conclusion follows. To disagree, you have to show which premise is false.

They say “It is better to bet everything on a single solution.”

Answer With so much uncertainty, betting everything on one scenario is risky. Broad strategies protect us against the unexpected.

Original text Luciano Carlos Cunha · Volume XII, p. 47 of the PDF edition · English translation

If our goal is for our decisions to have the best impact for all beings, we need to take their long-term impact into account58. In this regard, there are two main groups of strategies: broad and targeted.

Strategy Broad Targeted
What it consists of Trying to have the best possible impact across a wide variety of scenarios.Trying to have the best possible impact in specific scenarios.
Advantage They will have a positive impact in a great many scenarios, perhaps in almost all of them.They can have a very large impact if the scenario they address occurs.
Disadvantage This impact is smaller than that of targeted strategies when the scenario for which those are designed occurs.They may have a very small or even no impact if other scenarios occur.

The following tables show some examples of broad and :

Examples of broad strategies
TypeExample
Increasing consideration for sentient beings. (IX, ch. 9.3.1).
• Advocating the moral consideration of all sentient beings (focusing on the most neglected, such as aquatic animals and invertebrates, and new forms of sentience that may arise in the future).
• Addressing all causes of harm to sentient beings (whether those caused by their exploitation, those due to their situation in the wild as a result of natural processes, or others that may come to exist in the future).
• Promoting institutional changes that reinforce the changes in attitude achieved and make future regressions harder.
Increasing our capacity for action in the future (IX, ch. 9.3.2).
• Spreading concern for the long-term future and for s-risks (this will lead more people to work on these topics).
• Trying to increase the resources of people interested in preventing future suffering.
• Increasing our knowledge about the long term. This will allow us to: (1) better understand how to plan strategies; (2) identify strategies not yet considered; and (3) clarify which of the available strategies have the greatest potential.
Examples of targeted strategies
TypeExample
Focus on avoiding animal exploitation (IX, ch. 9.4.1).
• Preventing new forms of animal exploitation (for example, by banning new forms of exploiting invertebrates for consumption).
• Developing proteins and materials of non-animal origin to replace those of animal origin.
• Trying to prevent artificial intelligence from being used to improve the efficiency of animal exploitation.
Focus on avoiding the suffering of wild animals (IX, ch. 9.4.2).
• Creating and consolidating the field of welfare biology59.
• Establishing regulations for space exploration so as to avoid the intentional or accidental spread of life beyond planet Earth (even nonsentient life, since over a long time it could evolve and give rise to sentient beings).
Focus on consideration for new forms of sentience (IX, ch. 9.4.3).
• Trying to prevent the engineering of new forms of sentience, both biological and nonbiological.
• Trying to secure legal rights for nonbiological sentient entities.
• Pursuing a non-anthropocentric alignment of artificial intelligence (that is, ensuring that artificial intelligence does not have anthropocentric values).

Precautions when choosing longtermist strategies

We have seen some examples of strategies for preventing long-term suffering. Below are some precautions we should take when choosing them60:

Risk Precaution
Underestimating the complexity of the questionGiven the uncertainty about the future, remember that there is probably no single, conclusive answer. We should always remain open to revising our beliefs about which strategies are best, and be prepared to change strategy if necessary.
Cognitive biases may lead us to trust certain approaches too much and to dismiss others prematurely. We tend to neglect the possible negative effects of the approaches we initially sympathize with and the possible positive effects of the approaches we initially do not sympathize with.Remember that we may be biased in our assessments, cultivate epistemic humility, and always seek new information.
Certain approaches carry a high risk of leading people who have other priorities to reject reducing s-risksIt is better to advocate goals that can be accepted by a wide range of perspectives.

We will see more about this last point in the next section.
