The Essentials of Animal Ethics

A schematic summary of the main discussions

About this volume

By comparing the arguments of opposing positions, this book presents, in schematic and summarized form, a general overview of the main discussions in the field of animal ethics.

ISBN 978-65-01-77435-0 · 1st edition, October 2025. Original text by Luciano Carlos Cunha, translated into English.

In a nutshell

A schematic guide to the whole collection: each debate laid out in tables of "attempted justification" and "criticism," with pointers to where the topic is explored in depth. It is the best way in and the best review.

For kids

It is like the map of a huge park: it shows all the paths at once, so you can choose where you want to go.

You will learn

  • An overview of all the major debates in animal ethics
  • The most common objections and how they have been answered
  • The numbers on exploitation and on nature
  • The central concepts about the future and activism

It helps to have read: nothing. Start here if you want an overview.

Starting from scratch?

Every chapter has a simplified version, written for readers new to the topic. Read the simple version first, then the author’s full text.

Chapters

  1. ·
    Introduction

    Volume XII is a schematic guide to the other 11 volumes: each debate appears in tables of attempted justifications and criticisms, with an indication of where it is explored in depth.

    2 min
  2. 1
    Attempts to Justify Disregarding Animals

    The attempts to justify disregarding animals (species, soul, capacities, potential, group, biology, nature, tradition, ethics as a human invention or activity, self-protection, authority, degree of suffering, ecosystems) fail to explain why these criteria would be relevant.

    5 min
  3. 2
    The Case for Considering All Sentient Beings

    The main arguments for considering all sentient beings are the argument from species overlap, the argument from relevance, the principle of equal consideration, and the impartiality argument; the table also covers testing for speciesism, the benefit of the doubt, priorities, and desert.

    4 min
  4. 3
    The Debate on the Sentience Criterion

    The sentience criterion faces many criticisms (utilitarianism, anthropocentrism, animal-kingdomism, harms without suffering, invertebrates, biocentrism, the value of life, subjectivity, plants), and each one has been answered.

    5 min
  5. 4
    The Harm of Death and the Reasons Against Killing

    The attempts to justify killing animals painlessly (they do not understand death, have no desires about the future, have no intellectual pleasures, are replaceable, have weak psychological connectedness, would suffer anyway, have little time left, there is life after death) do not hold up.

    4 min
  6. 5
    Differences Between Environmentalism and Consideration for Animals

    Environmentalism and consideration for animals have different ultimate goals: one values certain configurations of ecosystems and species in themselves; the other, the well-being of sentient beings. Balance, biodiversity, and "looking at the whole" are not the same as caring for animals.

    5 min
  7. 6
    The Situation of Wild Animals and the Proposal to Help Them

    The proposal to help wild animals rests on the idea that the strength of our reasons depends on the magnitude of the harm, not its origin; and it answers the objections that wild animals live well, that destroyed eggs reduce the number of victims, and that newborns barely suffer.

    3 min
  8. 7
    The Debate on the Proposal to Help Wild Animals (Part 1)

    The table answers the most common objections to helping wild animals: outside the scope of ethics, arrogance, anthropocentrism, less human influence, freedom, dependence, paternalism, the value of suffering, indifferent nature, ecology, and environmentalist goals.

    11 min
  9. 8
    The Debate on the Proposal to Help Wild Animals (Part 2)

    The second set of objections to helping wild animals denies our responsibility or questions practicality; the table answers each one and includes a simulated debate on altruistic killing and the debate on expanding nature.

    18 min
  10. 9
    What Criteria Should We Use to Help Efficiently?

    To help effectively, we use criteria: seriousness (the number of victims and how bad it is for each one), tractability, neglect, size of the benefit, long-term effects, and tackling causes, not just symptoms.

    4 min
  11. 10
    Which Problems Affect the Largest Numbers of Animals?

    The 80 billion animals killed per year that activists cite are only mammals and birds, between 0.1% and 0.2% of the 34 to 54 trillion killed in exploitation; and animals in the wild, numbering 1 to 10 quintillion, make even these figures look small.

    2 min
  12. 11
    Key Concepts in the Debate on the Importance of the Future

    Key concepts about the future: s-risks (astronomical suffering), how they differ from x-risks, seriousness as severity times probability, speciesism and substratism, and the classification of s-risks as incidental, agential, or natural, known or unknown.

    3 min
  13. 12
    Identifying Risk Factors for S-Risks

    As in medicine, we can identify risk factors for s-risks without predicting the future in detail: disregard for nonhuman and nonbiological sentient beings, advanced technology, aversion to technology, lacking or ineffective efforts, polarization, and malevolent agents.

    3 min
  14. 13
    Longtermist Strategies: Broad and Targeted

    Longtermist strategies can be broad (a good impact in almost every scenario, such as increasing consideration for sentient beings) or targeted (a large impact in specific scenarios, such as regulating space exploration or aligning AI); in choosing them, we need humility and widely acceptable goals.

    3 min
  15. 14
    Changing Public Debate and Political Institutions

    Improving public debate and institutions is crucial for avoiding s-risks: thinking in nuances, discussing arguments rather than people, intellectual honesty, a broad-range approach, protecting democracy, representing all sentient beings; even small improvements would make a big difference.

    3 min
  16. 15
    Debates on Activism Strategies

    The table summarizes strategic debates in activism: veganism or reduction, reform or abolition, indirect or direct arguments, a broad focus or a focus on animals, technology or social change, the general population or influential people, behavior or attitude, controversial actions, social or institutional change.

    5 min
  17. 16
    Evaluating Different Strategies

    To evaluate strategies, we can use metacriteria: tackling causes, preventing backsliding, how much it has already been tried, track record, how many animals it benefits, how much it benefits each one, potential to make other changes easier, risks, cost-effectiveness, acceptance, alignment with other goals, ease, and expected success.

    2 min
  18. 17
    Conclusion

    The volume closes the collection with an overview of the main questions in animal ethics, offering an introductory foundation for anyone who wants to go deeper into the other volumes.

    1 min
