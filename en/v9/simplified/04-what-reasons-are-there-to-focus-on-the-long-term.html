What Reasons Are There to Focus on the Long Term?

In plain language, for readers who are new to the topic. About 2 min of reading.

What is this page? A simplified explanation written for this site based on Luciano Carlos Cunha’s text. It leaves out details, examples and sources. For the full argument, read the author’s original text.

On this page

  1. What longtermism is
  2. Why adopt a longtermist goal?
  3. How big the future is
  4. In short

What longtermism is

The word longtermism is used in several ways. The author separates two things:

New word

Longtermist goal: wanting to have the best possible impact on all of history, from now until the end of time.

New word

Practical focus: what we concentrate on day to day in order to reach a goal.

Two people can have the same longtermist goal and disagree about the focus:

  • long-term focus: thinking mainly about the effects of our decisions in the distant future;
  • short-term focus: thinking mainly about the effects now and in the near future, believing that's what helps most overall.

Someone with a short-termist goal, on the other hand, only wants the best impact now or in the near future.

Why adopt a longtermist goal?

The main reason is simple: if we care about all the beings we can affect, the question shouldn't be "how do we have the best impact today?" but "how do we have the best impact across all of history?"

How big the future is

The future could be incredibly long and contain vastly more beings than exist today. If our decisions can affect that future, even a small effect could reach an enormous number of beings.

Remember

If all beings matter, including those not yet born, then the distant future is a huge part of what matters.

In short

  • Longtermism means wanting the best impact across all of history.
  • You can have that goal with either a long-term or a short-term focus.
  • The main reason is that all future beings matter too.
