Volume IX · Advanced level

Ethics and the Future

What risks of future suffering are and how to prevent them

13 chaptersabout 2 hours of reading127 notes69 references

0 of 13 read

About this volume

This book presents the debate on ethical questions concerning the long-term future, especially risks of future suffering: what they are, how to identify them, and how to prevent them.

ISBN 978-65-01-55743-4 · 1st edition, July 2025. Original text by Luciano Carlos Cunha, translated into English.

In a nutshell

The future may contain far more sentient beings than the present, and far more suffering. This volume presents s-risks, temporal impartiality, longtermism, risk factors, broad and targeted strategies, and the role of politics and emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence.

For kids

The children who will be born a hundred years from now do not exist yet, but they will feel everything that happens to them. The same goes for the animals of the future. This book asks: what can we do today so that the future does not hold a gigantic amount of suffering?

You will learn

  • What s-risks are and how they differ from x-risks
  • Why the future matters just as much as the present
  • Which factors increase the risks of future suffering
  • How politics and AI fit into this discussion

It helps to have read: Volumes II and VIII.

Chapters

  1. 1
    Future Risks for Sentient Beings: An Introduction

    S-risks are risks of future suffering on a scale greater than all the suffering that has ever existed; they differ from x-risks (human extinction), their seriousness is severity times probability, and they can arise incidentally, agentially, or naturally.

    16 min
  2. 2
    Temporal Impartiality: What It Is and Why It Matters

    Temporal impartiality means giving the same weight to a given suffering no matter when it happens; temporal bias and the idea that future beings are merely potential do not justify disregarding them. Concern for the future must reject speciesism, and vice versa.

    8 min
  3. 3
    The Objection That Future Beings Are Merely Potential Beings

    Even those who deny a duty to bring new beings into existence have duties toward future beings, such as not causing them suffering; and the struggle for animals is already, in general, about future beings: those who would be born to suffer in exploitation.

    7 min
  4. 4
    What Reasons Are There to Focus on the Long Term?

    Longtermism can be a goal (having the best impact on all of history) or a practical focus (acting with the long term in mind); there are good reasons for the practical focus, and even those who prioritize the present have reasons to accept the goal.

    13 min
  5. 5
    Should We Focus on Preventing S-Risks or X-Risks?

    There are reasons to focus more on s-risks than on x-risks: attempts to justify focusing on x-risks because humanity is "essential" or because it attracts more resources tend to be speciesist; but one goal does not rule out the other.

    5 min
  6. 6
    Which Goals Should We Prioritize in the Long Term?

    Among the possible long-term goals, there are reasons to give extra weight to preventing suffering (and premature deaths): several theories accept this focus, and you do not need to be very pessimistic about the future to adopt it.

    16 min
  7. 7
    What Does the Focus on S-Risks Consist Of?

    Focusing on s-risks does not mean focusing on a few specific scenarios: future suffering may come from the sum of many risks; collectively, we should cover a broad range, even if each person specializes.

    3 min
  8. 8
    Identifying Risk Factors for S-Risks

    Even without predicting the future in detail, we can identify risk factors for s-risks: advanced technology, aversion to technology, disregard for nonhumans, lacking or ineffective efforts, polarization, and lack of security against malevolent agents.

    8 min
  9. 9
    Longtermist Strategies: Broad and Targeted

    Broad strategies aim for a good impact in almost every scenario; targeted ones aim for a large impact in specific scenarios. Broad examples: increasing consideration for sentient beings; targeted examples: preventing new forms of exploitation, establishing welfare biology, aligning AI in a non-anthropocentric way.

    5 min
  10. 10
    Why Focus on Consideration for Sentient Beings and on the Future?

    How we will affect future beings is rarely discussed; promoting consideration for all sentient beings is central to preventing future suffering, but there are risks in how it is done, and we do not yet know the most effective way; we need to keep an open mind.

    5 min
  11. 11
    Changing Public Debate, Forms of Activism, and Political Institutions

    Dysfunctional politics increases s-risks; to improve it, we should reduce tribalism, raise the standard of debate (discussing arguments, not people; intellectual honesty), adopt broad-range approaches, protect democracy, and seek representation for all sentient beings.

    13 min
  12. 12
    Shaping Emerging Technologies to Prevent Future Suffering

    Emerging technologies could multiply suffering: the chapter discusses shaping artificial intelligence with a focus on s-risks, avoiding indirect risks from AI, managing space colonization, and the risk of creating new forms of sentience.

    9 min
  13. 13
    The Influence Triad and Future Suffering

    The influence triad (an agent's intention, knowledge, and power) helps us assess the suffering that may result from decisions: agents who want to cause harm or are indifferent, and who have a lot of power, are the most dangerous; wanting to do good without knowledge or without power tends to be ineffective.

    28 min
